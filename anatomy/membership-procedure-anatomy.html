Membership and boundary procedure

The method drawn as a manual telephone exchange. Select a numbered part, run the pipeline, or step through cases N05 and N06.

    Not reported in the article

    • The total size of the archive.
    • How many neighbours were supplied per group, by whom, and within what window. Only N06-10 is named; the neighbour column therefore has no jacks except N06-10.
    • Which group belongs to which of the four posts. The trunks are drawn into one bus.
    • Comment-level membership states for N01, N04 and N07–N11, and which N02 comment is unresolved. The article places these in Supplementary Table S1.
    • The individuation rule that §3.5 says the study states.
    • Any count in the five counting units (tokens, sequences, occurrences, combinations, configurations).
    • The preliminary form's fields beyond "expanded boundary accounts and separate decision fields".
    • A narrower selection for N05. The comparator shows only the candidate and its extension.

    Where the machine misrepresents

    • Prosodic scope. Meaning spreading across wording is not a signal travelling down a wire.
    • Extensions. They are activated by a judgement that an alternative could change the explanation. The leads are clipped by hand; nothing trips automatically.
    • Derivation. A real exchange does not rewire itself by comparing logs. The layer is drawn behind the board.
    • Lamps. A reader may still read a lamp as the status of a comment. N06-10 carries two cords in two states to counter this.
    • Focus. The focus is a set of relations. The shaded band marks only the contextual window.
    • Jack order. Every comment gets a fixed position; the procedure treats export order only as a starting point.

    Review notes for the author

    Not part of the caption.

    1. Derivation, reapplication and adjudication use the same archive, analyst and cases, in a fixed reading order with full prior knowledge (§3.6). The plate shows this: the derivation layer and the board are fed by the same bus.
    2. §3.5 reads "The study states its individuation rule". The rule does not appear. Either state it or reword the sentence as a requirement.
    3. Five counting units are defined; no count is reported in any of them.
    4. Table 2 reports membership states for N02, N03, N05 and N06 only. A full tally needs S1.
    5. "Supplied neighbours" are undefined, yet they set the contextual window and produce the unresolved candidates.
    6. The origin of grouping fields is not described, though group labels are a selection criterion.
    7. Choosing the "nearest plausible" candidate lists four cues (address, lexical uptake, chronology, thematic similarity) without a priority or a criterion for plausibility.
    8. The order of the configuration threshold (§3.2) and membership assignment (§3.4) is not explicit. The pipeline follows §3.4.
    9. "Candidate" names the middle selection in §2.2 and Table 1, and the extension item in §3.3.
    10. Kiesling et al.'s scales and trained-annotation experiment were not reproduced, so the baseline is a qualitative reading.
    11. Selection before reading fixes order, not blindness: participants recur, E01's insult formula reappears and prior annotations exist.
    12. Extension triggers are counterfactual ("could change the explanation") with no test for "could".
